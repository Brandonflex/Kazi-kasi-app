## 1. THE ASSIGNMENT

You have been contracted to **reconstruct, rebuild and redesign** Kazi-Kasi — a job discovery platform for the Kenyan market — into something the category has never seen.

**Not a refresh. Not a reskin. A rebuild.**

The current product is a functional job feed with server-rendered HTML. It works. It does not inspire. Your mandate is to keep what works (the ranking algorithm, the freshness promise, the multi-source aggregation) and rebuild **everything else** around a new creative spine: **modern, creative, immersive, 3D-featured, fun, unique, niche.**

Think of it as the difference between a government noticeboard and a creative studio. We're building the studio.

---

## 2. THE VISION — IN ONE SENTENCE

> **Kazi-Kasi is the first job platform that feels like it was hand-built by someone who actually understands the Kenyan job market — warm, human, visually rich, spatially immersive — and that treats every job seeker like a person with a story, not a data point in a spreadsheet.**

This is not Indeed-with-a-coat-of-paint. This is not LinkedIn-with-green-instead-of-blue. This is a new category: a **job platform that respects the craft of design** and the dignity of the job seeker equally.

---

## 3. THE CREATIVE NORTH STAR

### Borrow from these principles (not these companies):

**Apple Human Interface Guidelines (2026)** — Purpose, agency, responsibility, familiarity, flexibility, simplicity, craft, delight. Clarity, deference, depth. Motion with purpose. Every pixel earns its place.

**Anti-AI Design Mandate** — If it looks like it was generated by Midjourney at 3am, burn it. No neon. No glowing orbs. No gradient backgrounds that look like a synthwave album cover. No generic "modern SaaS" layouts. If a Dribbble shot from 2024 looks like it, it's wrong.

**Kenyan Warmth** — This product lives in Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Kisii. It speaks Swahili and Sheng and English in the same breath. It understands what "ksh" means without explanation. It knows a matatu ride to CBD costs more than a bus to Thika. The design must feel local without being cartoonish.

**Hand-built Aesthetic** — Think: a designer who stayed up late because they cared. Think: editorial typography, unexpected layout choices, texture that comes from craft not a texture pack. Think: the opposite of "shipped by a growth team."

## 4. RESEARCH SYNTHESIS — THE 15 PLATFORMS

You must study these platforms. You must borrow from them. You must not copy them.

### TIER 1: THE GIANTS (study for scale and efficiency)

**1. Indeed (400M visits, 61% market share, $7.4B revenue)**
- **Borrow:** Salary upfront (the single most impactful design decision in job search). Filter chips horizontal, not sidebar. Two-column layout (list + detail). Stats bar at top.
- **Do NOT copy:** The beige-and-green corporate palette. The cluttered job cards. The fact that nothing has changed since 2019 visually.
- **Key insight:** Salary transparency isn't a feature — it's a trust signal. Make it the loudest thing on the card.

**2. LinkedIn (87M visits, 13% market share, $17.8B revenue)**
- **Borrow:** Profile-based matching. Network signals ("3 connections work here"). Professional credibility. Card modularity with tags. Save-to-tracker from search.
- **Do NOT copy:** The feed clutter. The blue. The "influencer" noise. The bloated nav.
- **Key insight:** Professional identity is an asset — use Google sign-in data to make the experience feel like it knows you, not like it's selling you.

**3. ZipRecruiter ($449M revenue, 89% gross margin)**
- **Borrow:** 81% mobile-first design. Fast bulk applying. One-post distribution concept. The "Apply" button is always one tap away.
- **Do NOT copy:** The subscription wall. The aggressive employer-side UX.
- **Key insight:** Speed is respect. Every extra click is a sign you don't value the user's time.

**4. Glassdoor (27M visits, $320M revenue, merged into Indeed 2026)**
- **Borrow:** Salary transparency (actual numbers, not estimates). Company culture insights. Interview question databases. Trust through transparency.
- **Do NOT copy:** The forced-account wall. The UI that got progressively worse. The review manipulation.
- **Key insight:** Information asymmetry kills trust. Give users what employers would prefer to hide.

**5. Jooble (34M visits, 5% market share)**
- **Borrow:** Clean search interface. Salary filters as first-class citizens. The absence of clutter — what they leave out is more important than what they include.
- **Do NOT copy:** Nothing — study them for restraint.

### TIER 2: THE SPECIALISTS (study for niche strength)

**6. JobStreet (16.6M visits, Asia Pacific)**
- **Borrow:** Regional localization that doesn't feel generic. Southeast Asian markets have similar trust dynamics to East Africa.

**7. Talent.com (14.6M visits)**
- **Borrow:** Salary comparison tools. Job alerts that don't spam. Clean typography.

**8. Hellowork (9.2M visits, fastest growing at 29% YoY)**
- **Borrow:** Mobile-first without being mobile-only. Fast UX. The growth comes from speed, not features.
- **Key insight:** They're growing because everyone else is slow.

**9. Monster (7.2M visits)**
- **Borrow:** Company profile pages. Career advice content as SEO moat.
- **Do NOT copy:** The brand has aged poorly. Study what not to do.

**10. FlexJobs (6.6M visits, curated paid)**
- **Borrow:** Curation as a feature. Every listing is hand-verified. Scam-free guarantee. Paid users trust the platform because the platform does work they can't do themselves.
- **Key insight:** Trust is the most defensible feature in job search.

### TIER 3: THE WORKFLOW INNOVATORS (study for efficiency)

**11. Mokaru (mokaru.ai — the efficiency benchmark)**
- **Borrow:** This is the single most important platform to study for workflow.
  - Browser extension saves jobs with one click
  - Auto-prep tailors resume to each job using only real experience (never fabricates)
  - Match score is TRANSPARENT — shows matching AND missing keywords
  - Application tracker: watchlist → saved → applied → screening → interview → offer → rejected
  - Follow-up reminders, contact tracking per application
  - Resume quality score with specific action items
  - Cover letter generation from real experience
- **Do NOT copy:** The resume builder itself (we're a job platform, not a resume tool). The browser extension (different product category).
- **Key insight:** The job search is a CRM problem. Most platforms treat it as a search problem. Kazi-Kasi should treat it as both.

**12. Wellfound/AngelList (3.8M visits)**
- **Borrow:** Startup culture positioning. Equity/salary transparency. The "who's hiring" energy.
- **Key insight:** Niche positioning beats generalist every time. We own Kenya.

**13. Welcome to the Jungle (France, high interview rates)**
- **Borrow:** Employer branding that's actually beautiful. Company pages that look like magazine spreads, not corporate PDFs.
- **Key insight:** If a company page is ugly, candidates assume the company is ugly.

**14. Seek (3.3M visits, Asia Pacific/Australia)**
- **Borrow:** Regional market dominance through localization. Don't try to be global — be the best in your geography.

**15. RemoteOK (752K visits, fully remote)**
- **Borrow:** The niche focus. Every listing is remote. The brand is the filter.

## 5. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS — THE NON-NEGOTIABLES

These are the functional patterns that MUST exist in the rebuild. They come from research, not opinion.

### 5.1 THE FEED (primary surface)

- **Two-column layout** (desktop): Job list left, context/filters right. Collapses to single column on mobile.
- **Salary on every card** — prominent, large, the first thing after the title. Format: KES 50,000 – 80,000/month.
- **Match breakdown** (Mokaru pattern): Show matching keywords (green) AND missing keywords (orange) on every card when user has a CV. Never fabricate — use only real CV data.
- **Application stage badge**: watchlist → saved → applied → screening → interview → offer → rejected. Color-coded pill on every card.
- **1-click apply**: If user has a saved CV, apply button is one tap. No forms, no friction.
- **Search bar**: Prominent, with instant suggestions pulled from CV skills, location, experience level.
- **Horizontal filter chips**: Not a sidebar. All jobs, Remote, Hybrid, Past week, Salary shown, Easy apply.
- **Stats bar at top**: Jobs matching, Live jobs, Closing soon, Applied — like Indeed's top metrics row.
- **Freshness indicator**: Every card shows when it was last checked. Stale jobs are marked.
- **Source attribution**: Show which source(s) the job came from (BrighterMonday, Fuzu, company site, etc).

### 5.2 THE APPLICATION TRACKER (Mokaru pipeline)

- **Full pipeline view**: Visual board (Kanban or list) showing all applications across stages.
- **Per-application data**: Notes, recruiter contact, follow-up reminder, salary as seen, resume version sent, source URL.
- **Funnel statistics**: Dashboard showing conversion rate at each stage.
- **Follow-up reminders**: Smart suggestions based on stage and time elapsed.

### 5.3 THE CV SYSTEM

- **Parse in under 3 seconds**: PDF, DOC, DOCX. Show progress during parsing.
- **Confidence scores**: For every extracted field (high/medium/low), user can verify.
- **Section detection**: Auto-detect education, experience, skills, contact info.
- **OCR for scanned PDFs**: Don't just fail — render the page and try OCR.
- **Match against job descriptions**: When user views a job, show transparent breakdown of what their CV has vs what's missing.

### 5.4 PERSONALIZATION (use the data you collect)

- **Google sign-in data**: Use given_name, family_name, picture, locale. Don't just store it — use it.
- **Personalized greeting**: "Hey Flex" not "Account."
- **Profile picture in navigation**: When signed in, show it.
- **Smart search suggestions**: Pull from CV skills, location, experience level.
- **Recommended jobs**: "Because you have Django experience" — explain the recommendation.

## 6. DESIGN DIRECTION

### 6.1 THE CREATIVE SPINE

This is where the 3D/immersive/creative brief kicks in. The functional requirements above give you WHAT to build. This section tells you HOW it should feel.

**Spatial Design (3D)**
- The feed is not a flat list. It's a surface with depth. Cards exist in space — they have weight, elevation, shadow that responds to scroll position.
- Consider Z-depth layers: background texture, mid-ground cards, foreground modals. Parallax on scroll.
- Hero sections that breathe — not static banners, but living surfaces. Subtle motion that responds to cursor/touch.
- 3D is not decoration. It's hierarchy. Use it to tell the user what matters.

**Typography as Architecture**
- Big, confident display type for headlines. Serif or editorial sans. Not Inter. Not Roboto. Something with character.
- Body type that's legible at 16px+. Line-height 1.6+. Let text breathe.
- Use type weight for hierarchy, not just size. A 600-weight headline at 24px can be more impactful than a 700-weight at 32px.
- Kenyan context: Consider a typeface that works well for Latin characters but doesn't feel imported. Test for Sheng readability.

**Color: Warm, Earthy, Confident**
- Primary: A deep, rich tone that feels like soil or wood, not like a tech brand. Think: #2C1810 (deep brown), #1A1A2E (night blue), #3D2B1F (espresso).
- Accent: Something alive. Coral, terracotta, saffron. Not electric blue. Not neon anything.
- Background: Warm off-white (#FAF7F2, #F5F0E8) — never pure white. White is hospital. Warm is home.
- Status colors: Green for success (sage, not lime). Amber for warning (honey, not caution tape). Red for danger (brick, not alarm).

**Texture & Material**
- Film grain: subtle, not noisy. It breaks up the flatness of digital.
- Paper-like surfaces: Cards feel like they're made of something. Subtle texture, subtle shadow, subtle response to hover.
- No glassmorphism unless it serves a function (backdrop blur for overlays, yes; decorative glass, no).

**Motion**
- 200-300ms for UI transitions. Fast enough to feel responsive, slow enough to feel intentional.
- Ease-out curves. Things arrive with momentum and settle naturally.
- Scroll-triggered reveals: cards fade up as they enter viewport. Stagger by 30ms for lists.
- Micro-interactions: Save button compresses on click. Match score fills like a progress bar. Stage badge pulses subtly when updated.
- No motion for motion's sake. Every animation answers: "What is this teaching the user?"

**Layout: Editorial, Not Corporate**
- Asymmetric grids. Not everything needs to be centered and symmetrical.
- Generous whitespace. Let the design breathe.
- Mixed media: text alongside imagery alongside data visualizations. Break up the monotony of "card, card, card."
- Consider a magazine-style homepage: hero feature, secondary stories, data spotlight.

### 6.2 THE ANTI-PATTERNS — WHAT TO AVOID

These are hard constraints. If your design includes any of these, it's wrong:

- ✗ Neon colors or glowing effects
- ✗ "AI-powered" language in the UI
- ✗ Generic "modern SaaS" layouts (hero → features → pricing → CTA)
- ✗ Rounded corners on everything (use radius intentionally)
- ✗ Gradient backgrounds that look like a crypto landing page
-  Stock photos of diverse people high-fiving
- ✗ Cartoon illustrations of people at computers
-  The word "empower" anywhere
- ✗ "Revolutionizing" or "disrupting" or "transforming"
- ✗ Pure white backgrounds (warm off-white only)
-  Roboto, Open Sans, Poppins as primary typeface (find something with more character)
- ✗ A nav bar that looks like every other SaaS product
- ✗ Cards that all look identical (vary by stage, importance, freshness)
- ✗ A mobile experience that's just the desktop shrunk

**Study these for craft, not to copy:**

- **Aesop.com** — Editorial layout. Restraint. Typography as design.
- **Linear.app** — Motion craft. Micro-interactions that feel physical.
- **Arc Browser** — Spatial design. 3D depth used for hierarchy, not decoration.
- **Stripe.com/docs** — Information design at scale. Complex data made beautiful.
- **The New York Times digital edition** — Editorial hierarchy. Mixed media.
- **Apple.com/product pages** — Scroll storytelling. Product as hero.

## 7. KENYAN CONTEXT — THE LOCAL LENS

This product serves a specific market. The design must reflect that without being patronizing or cliché.

**Understand:**
- 80%+ of Kenyan internet users are mobile-first. Design mobile-first, then enhance for desktop.
- Data is expensive. Performance is a design feature, not an engineering afterthought. Every KB matters.
- The job market is relationship-driven. Trust signals matter more than feature lists.
- "Ksh" is understood. "KES" is formal. Use the right register for the context.
- Counties matter. "Nairobi" is not the same as "Nakuru" is not the same as "Mombasa." Location is identity.
- The informal economy is real. Not every job has a formal title. Design for ambiguity.
- WhatsApp is the default communication channel. Consider integration points.
- M-Pesa is how people get paid. Consider salary display that maps to real purchasing power.

**Do NOT:**
- Use safari animals, maasai patterns, or "African" stock imagery
- Assume the user is in Nairobi (serve all 47 counties equally)
- Design for high-speed fiber (optimize for 3G/4G realities)
- Use English-only copy (Swahili and Sheng have their place)

## 10. CONSTRAINTS & GUARDRAILS

**Technical:**
- Server-rendered HTML (Python/Flask backend). No React/Next.js. This is intentional for performance and simplicity.
- CSS-only animations where possible. JS only when needed.
- SVG for icons and simple illustrations. No heavy raster assets on critical path.
- 3D elements must be GPU-efficient. Consider WebGL sparingly. Pre-baked where possible.
- All assets must be cachable. No uncachable JS blobs.

**Brand:**
- Name: Kazi-Kasi (Kiswahili: "fast work"). Pronounced "Kah-zee Kah-see."
- Tagline: TBD by this creative process. Do not use "your next career move" or any generic HR language.
- Logo: Existing logo can evolve but must remain recognizable.

**Scope:**
- Desktop (1200px+) and mobile (320-768px) are the primary targets.
- Tablet is secondary. Don't design for it first.
- Dark mode is optional in v1. If attempted, it must be a first-class design, not an inverted light mode.

**Timeline:**
- Phase 1: 1 week
- Phase 2: 2 weeks
- Phase 3: 2 weeks
- Phase 4: 1 week
- Total: 6 weeks from kickoff to handoff

## 11. DECISION FRAMEWORK

When you're stuck between two directions, use this framework:

1. **Does it serve the user's goal?** (Find a job. Apply quickly. Track progress.) If no, cut it.
2. **Does it build trust?** (Salary shown. Source attributed. Freshness dated.) If no, reconsider.
3. **Does it feel hand-built?** (Craft, texture, intentional type.) If no, push further.
4. **Does it work on a KES 15,000 Android phone on 3G?** If no, optimize.
5. **Would a designer at Apple or Pentagram be proud of this?** If no, raise the bar.
